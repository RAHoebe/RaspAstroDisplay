Catalog and astronomy

OpenNGC

Deep-sky positions, sizes, position angles, V magnitudes and aliases come from OpenNGC by Mattia Verga, under Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike 4.0. The selected derivative catalog contains 12,160 objects: 433 nebulae, 935 clusters/associations and 10,792 galaxies/groups. It converts coordinates to decimal degrees, adds type groups and some Dutch names, and excludes duplicate, nonexistent and single-star entries. The derivative remains CC BY-SA 4.0. Skyfield & JPL

Skyfield calculates topocentric apparent positions and astronomical events using the JPL DE421 ephemeris. Moon phase and angular size are calculated locally. The Moon drawing is schematic. Planet disk diameters use current distance and published equatorial radii; Saturn’s size indicator excludes its rings.

Survey images & comparison

DSS2 / CDS hips2fits

The default deep-sky reference is DSS2/color, accessed through hips2fits, developed at CDS, Strasbourg. The Digitized Sky Surveys were produced at the Space Telescope Science Institute under NASA grant NAG W-2166, using photographic plates including material from the Palomar Oschin Schmidt and UK Schmidt telescopes. Northern POSS material is associated with Caltech; UK Schmidt material has the respective UK and Anglo-Australian rights holders. This non-profit project acknowledges those institutions. DSS images are copyrighted: MAST permits research, educational and other non-profit uses with acknowledgment; commercial use requires the copyright holders’ written permission.

CDS hips2fitsDSS acknowledgmentsDSS copyright & data use

Viewer cutouts use explicit ICRS/TAN projection, catalog center, north up and east left. The website’s NGC 7000 reference has been resampled into the solved personal photograph’s WCS, including its orientation. Survey pixels are geometrically reprojected; the project does not claim a matched exposure, color response or resolution. These reference images retain their source rights.

Telescope field specifications

Nominal fields were checked against manufacturer specifications on September 13, 2026. They describe one telephoto exposure, without mosaics.

  • DWARF II ≈ 3.19° × 1.79°: derived from 100 mm focal length, 3840 × 2160 pixels and the Sony IMX415’s 1.45 μm pitch, using 2 atan(sensor side / (2 × focal length)). Sources: DWARFLAB and Sony IMX415 datasheet.
  • Seestar S50 Pro ≈ 1.37° × 2.44°: derived from the official 2.8° diagonal field and 2160 × 3840 aspect ratio, using half-angle tangents. Sources: ZWO product specifications and Seestar’s diagonal-FOV comparison.

These are calculated nominal fields, not measurements of every delivered crop. Stabilization, stacking and cropping affect usable edges. Custom profiles accept 0.01–20° per side. Raspberry Pi, DWARF and Seestar names identify compatible equipment; this independent project is not endorsed by their manufacturers.

What the estimates mean

Night selection and altitude

The coming or remaining night is the first continuous interval with the Sun below −6° in the next 24 hours. Deep-sky targets qualify when sampled above 0° during that interval. Samples are ten minutes apart, so a very short horizon passage can be missed. Future catalog selection uses apparent RA/Dec and local sidereal time; detailed target tracks use topocentric apparent positions. Venus, Jupiter and Saturn require at least 15° altitude and 20° solar separation. The Moon remains reachable regardless of altitude.

Recommendation score

The heuristic combines 40% altitude/duration, 30% frame coverage and 30% brightness, then penalizes low or tiny targets. For night recommendations, altitude uses 70% peak height and 30% longest continuous interval above 30° (up to two hours). Frame scoring considers both object axes against the frame, preferring useful coverage with margin. No actual camera orientation is assumed.

Brightness uses known V magnitudes and, for extended nebulae/galaxies with sizes, an approximate mean surface brightness: m + 2.5 log10(π × major × minor × 900), with axes in arcminutes and the result per square arcsecond. This is an estimate over catalog area, not a measured brightness profile. A cluster-with-nebulosity magnitude may include stars; it does not isolate the gas. Dark nebulae and missing magnitudes need different treatment and receive explicit uncertainty labels. OpenNGC’s B-band surface-brightness field is not mixed with V magnitudes.

Imaging windows

Suggested windows last 30–120 minutes, with the target at least 30° high and the Sun below −12° at every sample. Scoring considers mean height, duration, Moon altitude/separation/illumination and cloud forecasts. Missing weather is neutral (50% in scoring) and marked uncertain. Recommendations do not model obstructions, seeing, transparency, light pollution, filters or exposure strategy.

Size and percentage

Object extents come from catalog measurements; faint structures can extend beyond them. Where the minor axis is absent, the supplied diameter is treated as circular. Unknown sizes are labeled, with a context view instead of an invented percentage. Coverage is longest object dimension divided by longest frame edge, not area coverage or guaranteed fit.
